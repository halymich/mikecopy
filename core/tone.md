# Tone, when there is no saved voice

Voice is who is talking. Tone is how they sound in this piece. A saved voice already fixes most of
the tone, so this file is for the one-off piece with no voice on record: a toast, a story, a
thank-you note, a first post for a product that has no voice yet.

## The interview

One question batch, never more. Offer choices, not blanks, because people pick well and describe
badly. Four axes cover almost everything:

| Axis | One end | Other end |
|---|---|---|
| Distance | Formal: full sentences, no contractions, titles | Close: contractions, first names, asides |
| Temperature | Cool: facts, restraint, understatement | Warm: feeling named out loud, generosity |
| Energy | Calm: long sentences, few exclamations | Punchy: short lines, momentum, verbs up front |
| Humour | None | Open: jokes are part of the point |

Ask where on each axis this piece sits, plus two facts no axis captures: who reads it, and one
thing it must not sound like. The "must not" answer is worth more than the other five.

## Named styles

When the owner names a style, map it to the axes rather than to a stock register. These are
starting points, and the piece's specifics override them.

- **Plain and warm** (most product and marketing): close, warm, calm, a little humour. Short
  sentences, real nouns, no hype.
- **Confident and direct** (pitches, landing pages for experts): close, cool, punchy, no humour.
  Claims with numbers attached; concessions stated flatly.
- **Editorial** (articles, essays): formal-leaning, cool, calm, dry humour. Longer sentences
  that earn their length, a clear argument, one real opinion.
- **Playful** (social posts, invitations, kids, parties): close, warm, punchy, open humour. The
  joke is specific to this occasion or it is not a joke.
- **Formal** (legal notices, official letters, condolences): formal, warm or cool as the
  occasion needs, calm, no humour. Formal is not stiff: plain words, short paragraphs.
- **Luxury or ceremonial** (weddings, high-end products): formal, warm, calm, no humour. Restraint
  does the work. Fewer adjectives, not grander ones.

A style name is never a licence for its clichés. "Playful" does not mean exclamation marks and
emoji, and "luxury" does not mean "curated", "bespoke" and "elevated". The rules apply in every
style.

## Record it

State the tone you used in the report, as the four axis positions plus the "must not". If the
owner likes the result and the piece will recur (a newsletter, a product), offer to save it as a
voice record so the next piece starts there.
