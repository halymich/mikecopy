# personal

Writing as the owner: emails, LinkedIn posts, pitches, bios, announcements, replies. The reader
will believe a person wrote it, so it has to sound like that person, not like a competent stranger.

## 1. Their voice, from their own words

Read the owner's personal voice file (see [../core/voice.md](../core/voice.md) for where it
lives). If none exists, ask for three to five samples they wrote themselves, of the kind you are
about to write, and build the profile from those. Show them the profile before saving it. Never go
reading their mail, messages or files to learn how they write.

Different kinds sound different even from one person. Record per kind when the samples show it: a
LinkedIn post and an email to an investor rarely share a register.

## 2. Before drafting

- **Who is reading**, and what they already know about the owner.
- **The one thing this should make them do or think.** One. An email that asks for two things gets
  the easier one.
- **The facts.** Dates, numbers, names, what was agreed. Ask for any you do not have. Never fill a
  gap with a plausible guess: a wrong date in a personal email is a real problem for a real person.

## 3. By kind

- **Email.** The ask or the news in the first two lines. Subject says what it is about in words
  the reader would search for later. Sign-off matches the samples, not a template.
- **LinkedIn and social.** One idea. The first line has to work alone, because it is all that shows
  before "more". No emoji bullets, no one-sentence-per-line drama unless the samples do it, no
  closing question bolted on to farm replies. A specific story beats a lesson; if there is a
  lesson, the story should make it without being told.
- **Pitch.** What it is, who it is for, the proof, the ask. Numbers the owner can defend in the
  meeting and nothing they cannot.
- **Bio.** Third person unless told otherwise. What they do, for whom, one proof point, one human
  detail. Two lengths: one line and one paragraph.
- **Hard messages** (declining, apologising, chasing a debt). Say the thing in the first sentence,
  give the reason once, say what happens next. Warm is fine. Vague is not.

## 4. Never send

This skill drafts. Sending, posting and scheduling are the owner's, every time, even when a tool to
do it is available. Hand back the text ready to paste.

## 5. Verify

Pipe the draft to `lint.mjs --stdin --voice <personal voice file>`. Then reread it as the recipient:
would they believe the owner wrote this? If a phrase is one the owner has never used in any sample,
change it.
